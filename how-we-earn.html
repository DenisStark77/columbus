Disclosure

How we earn.

A site about other people's land should say plainly where its makers stand. Here it is.

Our own lots

We own lots 47 and 123. Lot 47 is where we live. If either is ever offered on this site, the listing will say "our lot", and we are the seller.

What we are paid for

  • Orientation calls, visits and guesthouse stays — paid by the visitor.
  • Lot reports — paid by whoever orders them, buyer or owner.
  • Listings priced above the comparable-sales range — paid by the seller. Listings within the range are free.
  • On some lots, a fee or a share of the price from the seller if a sale closes. When we show you a lot we would earn from, we tell you before you see it.

Prices on this site are guides. We agree the exact amount with you personally; nothing is charged through the site.

Three roles, always labelled

Every listing carries one of three labels, in the card and in the list:

  • Our lot we are selling our own land.
  • We represent the seller the owner pays us a fee or a share of the price.
  • Platform only an owner's or agent's listing; we are not part of the deal and earn nothing from it.

A listing gets the line "verified against the registry on" a date only after we have read the parcel's extract again on that date: the seller is the registered owner, and there are no mortgages, liens, court orders or notes.

What we are not

We are not the Columbus Heights HOA and do not speak for it. We are not licensed agents or lawyers. We do not hold money: a purchase is paid through your lawyer or an escrow of your choosing.

What you can rely on

Everything on this site rests on public registry records and real deeds, with the date each was read and the method described on the page "How we count". Where a figure comes from a small sample, the number of sales is shown next to it. Where we earn, it is written here.

Why a map

Over five years about a hundred families visited us. Two bought lots. We earned nothing from any of it, and the stories about problem lots kept circulating because nobody had put the registry on a map. This site is our answer: facts with dates, a fair range for every parcel, and modest pay for the work of keeping it current.
